AZARO — Menswear, pay on delivery

Turning an Instagram-only menswear store in Rabat into a phone-first shop where customers order in four fields and pay at the door. I designed it end to end, from the kickoff questionnaire to a Shopify-ready design system.

02 Overview

A store that sold on Instagram but couldn't take orders

AZARO is a men's streetwear and sportswear store on Avenue Al Kifah in Rabat. It sells sets, puffers, fleeces and knitwear at 99 to 249 DH, in sizes M to XXL. Every sale started on Instagram: a Reel filmed in the store, the price written on the video, then a DM or a WhatsApp message.

That meant every order needed a person to answer, confirm the size and arrange delivery by hand. Customers who browsed at night, or outside Rabat, often never got an answer.

The goal was not just a website. It was a working sales process: a shop that takes real orders on its own, in a market where most people pay cash on delivery.

03 Diagnosis

Real constraints, clear direction

✕ Constraints

  • Phone first. Almost all traffic arrives from Instagram and TikTok, on mid-range Android phones.
  • Cash on delivery. A refused parcel costs more than a lost sale, so trust has to come before style.
  • No brand guidelines. The only identity was a backlit shop sign and a black-and-white store.
  • Strict platform rules. No third-party brand names in product names, photos, tags or ads.
  • Lean budget. One launch budget for the Shopify plan, the domain and the first ads.

✓ Design Direction

  • Make three things obvious on every screen: the price, the size, and that you pay on delivery
  • Keep what already worked on Instagram: the big readable price, sizes up front, the real store as proof
  • Black is the brand: a quiet neutral system where colour comes only from the clothes
  • A checkout short enough to finish one-handed, with WhatsApp as the safety net at every step

A catalogue built from what the store actually sells

Eight menu entries. Promo sits in black, and Petits prix (under 150 DH, under 250 DH) gets its own entry, because price is the main reason people buy. Dashed categories wait for confirmed stock.

07 Key Decisions

Every screen answers a cash-on-delivery doubt

01 · Announcement bar

“Pay on delivery · Delivery all over Morocco”, stated once

Answers the first question of COD shoppers without repeating it on every block.

02 · Homepage

New arrivals right after the hero

Returning Instagram followers come for the latest drop, so it sits where they look first.

03 · Product card

One fixed card: photo and badge, name, price

Easy to scan in a two-column grid. New, Promo and Sold out read at a glance.

04 · Product page

One black button, nothing between size and “Add to cart”

Keeps attention on the one action that sells. WhatsApp stays a secondary link.

05 · Cart drawer

“To pay on delivery: 378 DH”

No surprise at the door, so fewer refused parcels. A bar shows what's left for free delivery, and removing an item gives a 5-second Undo.

06 · Checkout

Four fields, no account

Name, WhatsApp number, city, address. The phone field accepts 06, 07 or +212, with or without spaces.

07 · Confirmation

A real delivery date and a line in Darija

“We'll call you before shipping”, plus numbered next steps. The flow ends on reassurance and sets up the WhatsApp confirmation.

08 · Size guide

One table per product family, with an “Ask on WhatsApp” fallback

Sizing doubt is the main reason people hesitate on clothes. A human answer is one tap away.

08 On the phone

The look panel becomes a bottom sheet

On phones the outfit stays on stage and the pieces slide in as horizontal cards, with one button that adds the whole look and shows its price.

Below sit the trust strip (24–72h delivery, 14-day exchange, the Rabat store) and the full product grid with category chips.

09 Design System

Seven neutrals. Black is the brand.

No brand hue. Colour comes only from the clothes, and green or red appear only for success and error.

Aa Type

Montserrat 600 in tracked capitals echoes the shop sign. Inter keeps prices readable on cheap Android screens. IBM Plex Sans Arabic powers the Arabic storefront.

◻ Rules

Square corners, 1px lines, no shadows. 44px tap targets and WCAG 2.1 AA contrast. A pill shape is reserved for WhatsApp.

7 Components

Logo, Button, Badge, ProductCard, SizeSelector, TrustList and TextField, each mapped directly onto a Shopify Dawn section.

Photo direction. One framing for the whole catalogue: the item on a black hanger in front of the store's grey wall, then worn, then a detail shot. Prices live in the interface, never on the image.

10 Outcome

From DM-only selling to a buildable shop

In six days the store went from taking orders by hand to a complete, documented shop design: brief, moodboard, sitemap, eight wireframes, a homepage prototype and a design system ready for Shopify Dawn.

What I learned

In a cash-on-delivery market, trust is the conversion lever. Showing the real store, the amount to pay at the door and a WhatsApp follow-up does more than any visual effect.
